How to choose a jurisdiction

  • Your activity decides which authority applies. A holding structure and a regulated payment business are treated differently.
  • Substance expectations differ. Some jurisdictions expect a local director, office or staff, and that cost is extra.
  • Fees differ across the minimum to start, company formation and licensing support. Compare the total, not just one line.
  • Banking relationships vary. We offer introduction support only and cannot guarantee that a bank will open an account.

Questions

Is company formation the same as a licence?

No. Company formation and licensing support are separate services. Whether you need a licence depends on your activity, so confirm it with local counsel before you commit.

Do I need a local director?

Requirements differ by jurisdiction and by activity. We confirm them during the scope review, before you pay anything beyond the minimum.

How long does formation take?

It depends on screening, registry processing and how complete your documents are. The written quote gives an estimate for your case.

Facts about each jurisdiction

General information to help you compare the four jurisdictions. Laws and regulators change, so confirm the current position with local counsel before you commit.

About Saint Lucia

CapitalCastries
CurrencyEastern Caribbean dollar, pegged to the US dollar
Legal systemEnglish common law
LanguageEnglish
Time zoneUTC-4, no daylight saving
Financial regulatorConfirm with local counsel for your activity

Company limited by shares is the usual structure for holding and trading companies. Every company needs a registered office in the country, and a registered agent where the law requires one.

Directors and shareholders can be individuals or corporate entities. Confirm residency and nationality rules during the scope review, because they can change the documents we need.

Local and international banks run their own checks before they open an account, so an account is never guaranteed, even after the company is formed.

Saint Lucia is part of the Organisation of Eastern Caribbean States (OECS). The Caribbean hurricane season runs from June to November, which can delay in-person steps such as signing and document collection.

Good to know: Plan for a local contact point and a registered address from the start. Ask which registers are public and which are restricted.

About Saint Vincent and the Grenadines

CapitalKingstown
CurrencyEastern Caribbean dollar, pegged to the US dollar
Legal systemEnglish common law
LanguageEnglish
Time zoneUTC-4, no daylight saving
Financial regulatorConfirm with local counsel for your activity

The country is the main island of Saint Vincent together with the Grenadines, a chain of smaller islands. Services such as couriers and notaries can be concentrated on the main island, so ask us where the documents will be signed.

Company limited by shares is the usual structure for holding companies. A registered agent and registered office are usually needed in the country. Confirm which statutory registers the agent keeps.

Like Saint Lucia, Saint Vincent is in the Organisation of Eastern Caribbean States (OECS) and shares the Eastern Caribbean dollar. Banking is run by local and international banks that make their own decisions.

The hurricane season runs from June to November, so plan signing and document collection outside those months where you can.

Good to know: Ask early about courier and notary arrangements, because they affect how long original documents take to reach you.

About Mauritius

CapitalPort Louis
CurrencyMauritian rupee
Legal systemMixed: civil law from the French tradition, with English common law elements
LanguageEnglish is official; French is widely used
Time zoneUTC+4
Financial regulatorFinancial Services Commission (FSC)

Mauritius sits in the Indian Ocean. Its business day overlaps with the afternoon in Europe and the morning in Asia, which helps with calls across both regions.

Regulated financial and investment activity is supervised by the Financial Services Commission. Expect the regulator to look closely at local management, governance and substance.

Mauritius has a network of double taxation treaties. Whether a structure can use them depends on its facts, its substance and the rules in force at the time, so tax advice from a qualified adviser is essential.

A company that needs a local bank, administrator or auditor should ask us early, because these providers run their own checks on the client and its activity.

Good to know: Build local substance into the plan from the start. Regulators judge the real activity, not the paperwork alone.

About Seychelles

CapitalVictoria, on the main island of Mahé
CurrencySeychellois rupee
Legal systemMixed: civil law and English common law
LanguageEnglish, French and Seychellois Creole are official
Time zoneUTC+4
Financial regulatorFinancial Services Authority (FSA)

Seychelles is an island nation in the Indian Ocean made up of more than one hundred islands. Most business and government services are on Mahé, the main island.

Regulated activity, including virtual asset and payment services, follows rules set in local law. Confirm which authority supervises your activity and what it expects before you apply.

Companies usually need a registered agent in Seychelles, who can keep statutory records and assist with filings. Confirm which records are public and which are restricted.

Seychelles and Mauritius both use the Indian Ocean time zone, so meetings with Mauritius-based providers are easy to schedule.

Good to know: Ask whether your activity needs a licence before you choose the structure. Changing the structure after formation is slower and more expensive.

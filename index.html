Licensing at a glance

When a licence is part of the plan

A licence is needed when your activity is regulated in the jurisdiction where you run it. Virtual asset services, payment services and some investment activities are common examples. Whether your activity is covered depends on the exact business model, so we start with a written pre-assessment.

  • We map your activity against the rules in the chosen jurisdiction before you commit.
  • We prepare the application pack: business plan, compliance policies and fit-and-proper files.
  • We coordinate with local counsel and the regulator, and we answer questions in writing.
  • We prepare a first-year filing calendar, so ongoing duties are not missed after approval.
Common reasons applications stall

What we check early so it does not happen to you

Unclear source of funds

Regulators and banks ask where the money comes from. A clear, documented answer saves months.

Weak compliance policies

Generic policies that do not match the business are a common reason for follow-up questions.

Ownership that changes

Changes in shareholders or directors during the process restart some checks.

No real substance

Regulators look for local management and governance, not only a registered address.

More questions

Answers before you enquire

Questions

Can I form a company without a licence?

Company formation and licensing support are separate services. A company can be formed without a licence, but any regulated activity needs the right authorisation first. Confirm your activity with local counsel.

Do I need a local director or office?

It depends on the jurisdiction and the activity. We confirm the substance expectation in the scope review, before you pay beyond the minimum.

How long does the process take?

Timing depends on screening, registry processing and how complete the documents are. The written quote gives an estimate for your case.

Can I change the structure later?

Changes are possible but they cost time and money. We recommend settling the structure during the scope review.

Who decides the licence outcome?

The regulator. We prepare and coordinate the application, and we tell you early if we see a risk.

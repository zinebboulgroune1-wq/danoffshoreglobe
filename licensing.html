Which activities need a licence

Whether you need a licence depends on the exact activity: for example, operating a virtual asset exchange, providing custody, or offering payment services. Each jurisdiction sets its own definitions and thresholds. Before you apply, we recommend a written opinion from local counsel on your specific model.

Before you pay

Licence approval, timing and any capital or substance requirement are decided by the regulator and local law. Ask us for a written scope and a written quote before you commit to any payment.

The exact quote is set after identity screening. Indicative fee ranges only. Prices exclude government, regulator, bank and capital costs and are confirmed in writing after identity and source-of-funds checks. We do not guarantee incorporation timelines, bank account opening or licence approval.

After approval: ongoing obligations

  • Annual returns, renewals and regulator fees on the dates the regulator sets.
  • Anti-money-laundering and counter-terrorist-financing reporting, and staff training.
  • Prompt notice to the regulator of changes in ownership, directors or activity.
  • Record keeping for the period the law requires, and audits where the regulator requires them.

Questions

Can you guarantee approval?

No. Approval, timing and conditions are set by the regulator. We prepare and coordinate the application, and we tell you early if we see a problem.

Do you open bank accounts?

We provide introduction support only. Bank decisions are made by the bank, and we cannot guarantee an account.

Who pays the regulator's fees?

The client pays them directly or reimburses them. They are listed as excluded from our fee in every quote.

Documents we usually prepare for an application

  • Business plan with a description of the activity, customers, flows of funds and risk controls.
  • Anti-money-laundering and counter-terrorist-financing policies, with a named compliance officer.
  • Fit-and-proper files for directors, controllers and key staff.
  • Technical or operational description, where the activity involves systems or custody.

Questions regulators usually ask

  • Who owns and controls the business, and where the money comes from.
  • How customers are verified and how suspicious activity is spotted and reported.
  • Where the business is run from, and who is responsible for each function.

What we need from you

  • A one-page description of the business: customers, products, geography and revenue model.
  • Who owns, controls and manages the business, with supporting identity documents.
  • Where the money comes from and how client money will move through the business.
  • Any previous regulatory history, refusals or investigations, disclosed in full.

Why the regulator matters in the timeline

Regulators set their own timing and may ask follow-up questions at any stage. Those questions are outside our control, so we plan the pack to answer the most common ones before they are asked.
